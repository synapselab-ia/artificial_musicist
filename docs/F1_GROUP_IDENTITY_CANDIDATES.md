# F1 — GROUP IDENTITY & NAME CANDIDATES

Status: **AWAITING USER APPROVAL**

This is a working decision document. Nothing in this file becomes canonical merely by appearing here.

## Proposed core identity

A seven-member male K-pop group built around **contrast**: intimacy versus scale, restraint versus release, vulnerability versus performance power. Each release may change genre substantially, but the group should remain recognizable through seven distinct vocal/rap personalities, emotionally coherent songwriting, strong hooks, deliberate dynamic escalation, and ambitious cinematic presentation.

The group should be capable of songs as restrained and emotional as `Last Train Home` and songs that are much heavier, brighter, stranger, or more performance-driven without losing its identity.

## Naming criteria

The official name should:

- work across multiple genres and eras;
- avoid locking the group into only nocturnal, sad, futuristic, or aggressive concepts;
- be easy to pronounce internationally;
- be visually strong in uppercase typography and logo systems;
- support a memorable fandom/branding system later;
- not depend on the number seven as a gimmick;
- avoid obvious similarity to existing major K-pop acts;
- survive a basic conceptual collision screen before approval.

## Candidate 01 — UNFRAME

**Pronunciation:** `uhn-frame`

**Core idea:** Refusing a fixed frame. The same seven people can be presented through different genres, emotions, aesthetics, and eras without being reduced to one image.

**Why it fits:** It directly expresses the project's genre flexibility and visual ambition. It also reinforces the idea that the members remain constant even when the frame around them changes.

**Logo potential:** Very high. Possible treatments include `UNFRAME`, `UN//FRAME`, broken rectangular borders, open corners, or a frame that never fully closes.

**Strengths:** concise, memorable, visual, modern, conceptually broad.

**Risk:** The word is already used by non-music art/technology projects, and `Unframe` also appears as song/album wording. A preliminary search did not surface a prominent current K-pop act using the exact name, but this is not a legal/trademark clearance.

**Producer assessment:** **Strongest overall candidate.**

## Candidate 02 — SHIFTLINE

**Pronunciation:** `shift-line`

**Core idea:** The line where something changes state. Every comeback can cross into a different sound or visual world while preserving the same group identity.

**Why it fits:** It captures motion, transformation, and threshold moments without forcing the group into one emotional tone.

**Logo potential:** High. `SHIFT/LINE`, a moving divider, displaced baselines, or a line that changes position between eras.

**Strengths:** energetic, performance-friendly, easy to pronounce, strong era system potential.

**Risk:** `SHIFTLINE` is used by at least one non-music game/product project. Preliminary screening did not surface an obvious established music act with the exact name. Not a legal/trademark clearance.

**Producer assessment:** **Best alternative if we want a more kinetic, title-track-ready identity.**

## Candidate 03 — VANTLINE

**Pronunciation:** `vant-line`

**Core idea:** A coined name built around `vantage` + `line`: seven points of view meeting along one shared trajectory.

**Why it fits:** It gives us a largely blank semantic canvas while still suggesting perspective, direction, and movement.

**Logo potential:** High. Strong uppercase geometry, `VANT/LINE`, a V-shaped perspective mark, or converging lines.

**Strengths:** distinctive, compact, flexible, does not dictate a musical genre.

**Risk:** Because it is coined, the meaning would need to be taught through branding. A domain marketplace currently lists `VantLine.com`; no obvious music-act collision surfaced in the preliminary screen. Not a legal/trademark clearance.

**Producer assessment:** **Best invented-name direction, but less immediately meaningful than UNFRAME.**

## Candidate 04 — EDGEFORM

**Pronunciation:** `edge-form`

**Core idea:** Identity shaped at the edge of change: emotional, musical, and visual extremes forming something recognizable.

**Why it fits:** The name supports both vulnerable and aggressive eras and has a fashion/editorial feel rather than a purely technological one.

**Logo potential:** High. Hard edges meeting fluid forms, asymmetric typography, angular monogram possibilities.

**Strengths:** strong visual language, mature tone, broad genre compatibility.

**Risk:** `Edgeform` appears in existing media/music metadata as a title, although the preliminary screen did not surface a prominent established artist using the exact name. Not a legal/trademark clearance.

**Producer assessment:** **Visually strong, slightly colder and less immediately memorable.**

## Candidate 05 — MOTIONCODE

**Pronunciation:** `motion-code`

**Core idea:** The members are the persistent code; the motion around them changes. Genre, styling, choreography, and era can move while the underlying group identity remains recognizable.

**Why it fits:** It maps unusually well to the project's technical goal of maintaining persistent fictional characters across changing releases.

**Logo potential:** High. Modular typography, coded member identifiers, motion trails, seven recurring symbols.

**Strengths:** conceptually coherent, modern, strong system/era potential.

**Risk:** `MotionCode` is already used by non-music technology/growth companies and appears in music-release metadata as a title. No major K-pop act surfaced under the exact name in the preliminary screen. Not a legal/trademark clearance.

**Producer assessment:** **Strong concept, but the most tech-brand-like name in the shortlist.**

## Ranking before user input

1. **UNFRAME** — strongest balance of meaning, memorability, visual identity, and genre freedom.
2. **SHIFTLINE** — strongest kinetic/performance identity.
3. **VANTLINE** — strongest blank-slate invented brand.
4. **EDGEFORM** — strongest editorial/art-direction identity.
5. **MOTIONCODE** — conceptually precise but slightly too technological.

## Decision rule

Do **not** canonize a name until the user explicitly approves it. If none of these feels right, run another naming round instead of forcing a selection.
