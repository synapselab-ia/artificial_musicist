# F6 — New Song Concept Round

Status: **AWAITING USER SELECTION**

This round starts the second canonical UNFRAME release from a clean concept stage. `Crash Test` is not being revived. None of the concepts below is canonical until the user explicitly selects one.

## Evaluation standard

Every candidate was screened against the `Last Train Home` benchmark without using the debut as a genre template. The concept must support:

- a concise, memorable title-world;
- a clear emotional or narrative engine;
- meaningful contrast between restraint and release;
- real functions for all seven canonical members;
- a bridge/final-section escalation that can change perspective rather than merely repeat;
- enough conceptual specificity to avoid generic K-pop writing;
- strong differentiation from the debut's breakup, train-platform, 108 BPM, nocturnal R&B-pop identity.

Scoring uses a 1–5 scale and evaluates the concept only, before sonic direction, architecture, hook, or lyrics are locked.

## A — DRESS REHEARSAL

### Core concept

People keep living as if the real version of their life starts later: after they are ready, after they stop making mistakes, after they finally become the person everyone expects. The song captures the moment UNFRAME stops rehearsing for an imaginary future and chooses the imperfect present instead.

The central metaphor is theatrical, but the emotion is not about fame or performing onstage. It is about refusing to treat real life like a practice run.

### Emotional engine

`self-control -> hesitation -> recognition -> deliberate risk -> release`

### Why it fits UNFRAME

- **VALE** can embody the controlled, rule-bound opening perspective.
- **NOEN** can make the private doubt feel human rather than slogan-like.
- **ELIAN** naturally lifts the transition from hesitation toward action.
- **SOREN** has a justified large-release role once the decision is made.
- **ROOK** can attack the idea of scripts, rules, timing, and perfection with technical rhythmic writing.
- **MIRO** can bridge spoken rationalization into melodic conviction.
- **EON** can represent the late sense of freedom rather than simply adding an unrelated high note.

### Strengths

- Very strong title and metaphor economy.
- Naturally supports escalating sections.
- Distinct from `Last Train Home` in subject, imagery, and emotional outcome.
- Can become an anthemic release without requiring generic "we can do anything" lyrics.
- Feels compatible with UNFRAME's core identity of breaking fixed frames.

### Main risk

If the writing abandons concrete rehearsal/stage-language too early, it could collapse into a generic self-empowerment song. The later lyric phase would need precise images and decisions rather than motivational slogans.

### Concept score

| Criterion | Score |
|---|---:|
| Memorability | 5/5 |
| Narrative / emotional engine | 4.5/5 |
| Seven-member functionality | 5/5 |
| Dynamic-payoff potential | 5/5 |
| Distinctness from debut | 5/5 |
| Originality / specificity | 4.5/5 |
| **Overall** | **4.8/5** |

**Assessment:** strongest all-around candidate and highest-confidence route to a second song that feels ambitious without copying the debut.

## B — HUMAN ERROR

### Core concept

A world built around optimization, perfect timing, clean profiles, algorithms, and predictable behavior keeps treating emotion as a defect. The song reframes the mistakes, impulses, contradictions, and irrational attachments that cannot be optimized as the proof that someone is actually alive.

The concept can be personal rather than dystopian: two people, or the members themselves, repeatedly make the "wrong" choice because the technically correct one feels less human.

### Emotional engine

`precision -> disruption -> shame -> redefinition -> defiant acceptance`

### Why it fits UNFRAME

- **VALE** can establish cold control and measured authority.
- **ROOK** has an obvious precision-versus-chaos rap lane without becoming merely a fast rapper.
- **MIRO** is ideal for the transition between mechanical phrasing and emotional melody.
- **NOEN** can carry the smallest, most vulnerable evidence of imperfection.
- **ELIAN** can make the first emotional opening feel brighter rather than heavier.
- **SOREN** can turn "error" into conviction at the main payoff.
- **EON** can create a late, almost overloaded upper-register release once the controlled system breaks open.

### Strengths

- Highly compatible with the name **UNFRAME** and the group's contrast-based identity.
- Gives rap and vocal members equally meaningful conceptual territory.
- Broad enough for multiple future sonic directions without dictating one now.
- Can feel contemporary without depending on temporary internet slang.

### Main risk

The concept is more abstract than `Last Train Home`. It will need one concrete human scenario in the architecture/lyrics phase so it does not become a collection of clever technology metaphors.

### Concept score

| Criterion | Score |
|---|---:|
| Memorability | 4.5/5 |
| Narrative / emotional engine | 4/5 |
| Seven-member functionality | 5/5 |
| Dynamic-payoff potential | 5/5 |
| Distinctness from debut | 5/5 |
| Originality / specificity | 5/5 |
| **Overall** | **4.75/5** |

**Assessment:** the most inherently "UNFRAME" concept of the round. Slightly harder to write perfectly than `DRESS REHEARSAL`, but potentially more distinctive if executed with a concrete story.

## C — NO SAINTS AFTER MIDNIGHT

### Core concept

During the day, everyone is polished, responsible, diplomatic, and carefully edited. After midnight, the masks come off—not as a crime or debauchery fantasy, but as permission to be messy, honest, impulsive, loud, flirtatious, contradictory, and fully present for a few hours.

The point is not "we are bad boys." The point is that perfection itself is exhausting.

### Emotional engine

`polished restraint -> invitation -> mask slipping -> collective release -> sunrise consequence`

### Why it fits UNFRAME

- **VALE** can make restraint sound dangerous before the release.
- **ROOK** gets a high-personality rap lane built around verbal control breaking down.
- **MIRO** can turn the invitation into something melodic and charismatic.
- **NOEN** can expose the honest motive underneath the bravado.
- **ELIAN** can brighten the transition into the communal hook space.
- **SOREN** can own the largest release moments.
- **EON** can make the final section feel euphoric rather than simply louder.

### Strengths

- Immediate title appeal and strong performance identity.
- Excellent contrast between daytime polish and nighttime release.
- High replay/performance potential.
- Very far from the debut's emotional premise.

### Main risk

This is the easiest candidate to execute badly. If the lyric phase relies on generic "crazy night / bad boy / party" language, it loses the exact psychological idea that makes the concept worthwhile.

### Concept score

| Criterion | Score |
|---|---:|
| Memorability | 5/5 |
| Narrative / emotional engine | 4/5 |
| Seven-member functionality | 4.5/5 |
| Dynamic-payoff potential | 5/5 |
| Distinctness from debut | 5/5 |
| Originality / specificity | 4/5 |
| **Overall** | **4.6/5** |

**Assessment:** best candidate if the priority is a more immediate, confident, performance-forward second release.

## D — AFTERIMAGE

### Core concept

One brief encounter is so visually and emotionally intense that it remains "burned" into perception afterward, like the image left behind after looking into a bright light. Even with closed eyes, new rooms, and new people, fragments of that person keep appearing in color, motion, and negative space.

This is not a breakup song. It is about the sensory persistence of attraction and memory before the narrator even knows what the encounter means.

### Emotional engine

`impact -> fascination -> sensory repetition -> fixation -> surrender to the image`

### Why it fits UNFRAME

- **NOEN** has a natural intimate entry point through small sensory details.
- **VALE** can ground the obsession with a darker low-register perspective.
- **MIRO** can move fluidly between thought and melody.
- **ROOK** can turn repeated visual fragments into rhythmic patterning rather than a conventional boast rap.
- **ELIAN** can brighten the image as the song expands.
- **SOREN** can own the emotional recognition that the memory has become unavoidable.
- **EON** can create literal "light" in the upper arrangement through late counter-lines and high-register escalation.

### Strengths

- Strong visual world and elegant central metaphor.
- Excellent space for vocal texture and member contrast.
- Romantic without repeating the debut's goodbye narrative.
- Potentially very cinematic.

### Main risk

Among the four, this has the highest chance of drifting back toward the debut's intimate/atmospheric comfort zone during sonic direction. If selected, the next phase should deliberately choose a different rhythmic and production identity.

### Concept score

| Criterion | Score |
|---|---:|
| Memorability | 4.5/5 |
| Narrative / emotional engine | 4.5/5 |
| Seven-member functionality | 4.5/5 |
| Dynamic-payoff potential | 4.5/5 |
| Distinctness from debut | 4/5 |
| Originality / specificity | 4.5/5 |
| **Overall** | **4.4/5** |

**Assessment:** strongest choice if the user wants another emotionally immersive song, but it requires the most discipline to avoid sounding like a spiritual sequel to `Last Train Home`.

## Round ranking

1. **DRESS REHEARSAL — 4.8/5** — strongest total package and safest high-quality second-release foundation.
2. **HUMAN ERROR — 4.75/5** — most distinctive expression of UNFRAME's identity; slightly more difficult to make concrete.
3. **NO SAINTS AFTER MIDNIGHT — 4.6/5** — strongest performance-forward route; must avoid generic bad-boy writing.
4. **AFTERIMAGE — 4.4/5** — strongest immersive/romantic route; must avoid sonic overlap with the debut.

## Recommendation

If the goal is simply **the best odds of reaching the user's `Last Train Home` satisfaction level again**, start with **DRESS REHEARSAL**.

If the goal is to make release two immediately prove that **UNFRAME itself has a recognizable creative identity beyond the debut's sound**, choose **HUMAN ERROR**.

No sonic direction, song architecture, hook, or lyrics should be canonized until the user selects the concept.
