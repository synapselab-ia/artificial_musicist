# VOCAL DNA

Status: **ROLE FRAMEWORK ONLY — MEMBER MAPPING PENDING**

The group will use seven distinct permanent member IDs. Exact mapping of specialties to members is not canonical yet.

## Required functional palette

The final member system should collectively support:

- a dark, resonant low vocal;
- an intimate softer vocal for close verses;
- a bright melodic lead vocal;
- a powerful main vocal capable of chest-dominant belts and emotional climaxes;
- a high-register vocalist for upper harmonies, counter-melodies, sustained notes, and ad-libs;
- a deep, controlled rapper with spacious cadence;
- a technical fast rapper with dense internal rhyme and rhythmic switches;
- a melodic rapper/singer bridge between rap and vocal sections.

Because there are seven members and more than seven useful functions, at least one or two members should be credible hybrids rather than every function becoming a separate person.

## Group-vocal principles

- Choruses should use layered group vocals selectively rather than flattening every member into a single unison sound.
- Final choruses should be able to add upper harmonies, octave doubles, counter-lines, rapper interjections, and ad-libs without obscuring the main hook.
- High notes should be saved when possible so they create genuine escalation.
- Rap sections must be written as performance, with cadence, pockets, internal rhyme, pauses, syncopation, and personality.

## Continuity rule

Once member mapping is approved, prompts and lyrics should reference member IDs directly (`M01`, `M02`, etc.) when useful. Functional labels such as `[High Vocal]` become secondary annotations, not substitutes for member identity.

## Technical limitation

Flow/Lyria may not reproduce identical synthetic timbres across independent generations. Canonical continuity therefore includes vocal role, register, delivery, phrasing personality, line assignment, and arrangement behavior even when exact timbral identity varies.
