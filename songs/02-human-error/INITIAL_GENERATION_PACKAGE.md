# HUMAN ERROR — INITIAL GENERATION PACKAGE

Status: **READY FOR FIRST FLOW / LYRIA GENERATION**

This is the practical first-generation package for the second UNFRAME release. It uses the approved development decisions and the complete `LYRICS.md` draft **without rewriting the lyric**.

The first generation is diagnostic. Do not keep adding instructions to chase theoretical perfection before hearing the result.

---

## FLOW / LYRIA — SOUND

Paste as the main sound/style direction:

> Contemporary seven-member male K-pop at about 114 BPM, combining industrial electro-pop, groove-driven pop, and restrained hip-hop. Start surgically controlled: dry sharply defined electronic drums, disciplined deep synth bass, clipped metallic/percussive synth stabs, deliberate negative space, narrow stereo image, minimal ambience, and precise rhythm. Gradually let the production become more human: increasing syncopation and swing, warmer harmonic layers, room ambience, subtle saturation, human-feeling percussion, wider vocals, and selective live-feeling textures. Keep the choruses strongly melodic and emotionally replayable. Chorus 1 should still feel controlled, Chorus 2 warmer and wider, and the Final Chorus should become the full organic-electronic release without turning into noise. Polished contemporary K-pop production, strong vocal/rap contrast, dynamic escalation, and clear male group identity.

## FLOW / LYRIA — DETAILS / PERFORMANCE INSTRUCTIONS

Paste as the detailed direction:

> Target approximately 114 BPM in 4/4 and roughly 2:57–3:05 total duration. Preserve the section order and lyrics exactly unless the generation system requires harmless formatting adaptation. Bracketed section/member labels are performance instructions and must not be sung aloud.
>
> The central production story is CONTROL -> FRACTURE -> HUMAN RELEASE. The track must begin dry, narrow, exact, and rule-bound. Do not use generic glitch effects to communicate “error.” Instead, let the arrangement itself progressively violate its early discipline through syncopation, warmth, stereo width, room sound, saturation, human percussion, vocal layering, and selective live-feeling contamination.
>
> INTRO / VERSE 1: sparse and controlled. M01 VALE should sound like a dark, resonant low male voice with deliberate space and authority. M03 NOEN should contrast him with intimate, close, breath-controlled singing. Avoid large pads or emotional overproduction this early.
>
> PRE-CHORUS 1: M05 ELIAN introduces the first genuine melodic and harmonic lift with a bright clean tenor. M06 MIRO should bridge rhythmic speech into melody naturally. Let the stereo field begin to open, but keep substantial headroom.
>
> CHORUS 1: M02 SOREN is the main emotional lead: full-bodied male power vocal with controlled grit and a strong melodic center. Make “I’d make the same mistake twice” and “Call it human error” the clearest memorable phrases. This first chorus is still disciplined; do not use the maximum vocal stack, biggest drums, or highest voice yet. “Human error” must sound emotional and human, never like a robotic system notification.
>
> ERROR TAG: short, memorable first production fracture around “Same mistake — Twice.” Use a stop/start or rhythmic displacement if useful, but avoid novelty glitch spam.
>
> VERSE 2 / RAP SEQUENCE: M04 ROOK needs crisp technical rap with excellent diction, internal rhythmic control, pocket changes, and clear consonants. Fast articulation is allowed, but do not rush or blur the words at 114 BPM. M06 MIRO follows as a genuine melodic-rap / vocal hybrid, not generic rap followed by generic singing. M01 VALE then creates contrast with an extremely sparse deep-rap punctuation: “Pen stops. / Slide the paper back. / Let the silence answer that.” Give those lines space.
>
> PRE-CHORUS 2: M03 NOEN should feel exposed and vulnerable rather than weak; audible human intimacy is desirable. M05 ELIAN then lifts the section melodically. Introduce more room, warmer harmony, and human-feeling percussion than in the first cycle.
>
> CHORUS 2: same central hook melody and identity, but now more intentional and human. M02 SOREN remains the core lead. Add wider harmony, warmer bass/drums, more organic rhythmic feel, and selective group layers. M07 EON may appear only as restrained upper harmony here; save his largest register and ad-libs for the final section.
>
> BRIDGE: change perspective, not just volume. Strip enough production away to expose the lyric. M03 NOEN should stay intimate; M01 VALE and M06 MIRO support the conceptual reversal. The emotional turn is: stop treating personal desire as a fault that must be corrected. Do not turn this into a generic motivational speech.
>
> BUILD / CONTAMINATION: the controlled system fails to restore order. Let live-feeling percussion, room sound, saturation, or a selective distorted guitar/live-drum texture enter as contamination rather than becoming the base genre. M05 ELIAN lifts, M04 ROOK adds a sharp rhythmic interjection, and M06 MIRO carries the transition into release.
>
> FINAL CHORUS: maximum emotional and production width while keeping the hook intelligible. M02 SOREN still owns the core melody. M07 EON finally receives the strongest upper-register role: bright high counterline, sustained upper harmony, and selective ad-libs around SOREN rather than replacing him. Add ensemble layers and rapper interjections only where they strengthen the hook. The production should now feel physically human, warm, wide, slightly less rigid, and impossible to return to its opening sterility.
>
> OUTRO: do not reset to the clinical intro. Leave human residue — room, breath, warmth, subtle noise, or natural decay. Keep M03 NOEN intimate and M01 VALE minimal. End with the emotional consequence of the choice rather than a triumphant victory pose.

## CRITICAL NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS

These constraints are part of the generation package:

> Do not make this atmospheric nocturnal R&B-pop like “Last Train Home.” Do not use train/breakup imagery or imitate the debut’s arrangement. Do not turn the track into generic cyberpunk, AI, dystopian, or video-game music. Avoid excessive glitches, robotic voices, system alerts, error beeps, vocoders, or digital-failure sound effects. Avoid a generic EDM festival drop. Avoid making every chorus identical. Avoid spending the biggest vocal belt or highest register before the final section. Do not flatten all seven performers into the same male voice or the same rap cadence. Do not make ROOK’s rap unintelligibly fast. Do not make VALE rap with ROOK’s cadence. Do not make MIRO sound like two disconnected performers between rap and singing. Do not make SOREN and EON interchangeable climax voices. Do not over-sing NOEN’s intimate sections. Do not reduce the story to reckless romance. Do not rewrite the lyrics or replace the central hook.

## FIRST-GENERATION SUCCESS CRITERIA

Do **not** expect the first generation to be perfect. Keep it if the core is strong.

Evaluate the first result primarily on:

1. whether the chorus hook is immediately memorable;
2. whether Chorus 1 -> Chorus 2 -> Final Chorus audibly progresses from control toward humanity;
3. whether ROOK’s technical rap is clear and rhythmically convincing;
4. whether VALE, ROOK, and MIRO feel meaningfully distinct;
5. whether SOREN owns the chorus while EON clearly escalates the final section;
6. whether NOEN’s exposed moments remain intimate and emotionally credible;
7. whether the bridge genuinely changes perspective;
8. whether the production avoids generic glitch/robotic shorthand;
9. whether the outro retains human warmth rather than resetting to sterile control;
10. whether the complete song feels coherent and replayable around the ~3-minute target.

If most of these work, preserve the generation and diagnose surgically rather than immediately regenerating from scratch.
