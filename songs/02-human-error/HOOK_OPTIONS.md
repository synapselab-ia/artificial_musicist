# HUMAN ERROR — HOOK OPTIONS

Status: **AWAITING USER SELECTION**

This round defines the central hook direction only. The selected concept is **HUMAN ERROR**, the sonic direction is **PRECISION BREAKS INTO BLOOD**, the song architecture is **CONTROLLED FRACTURE**, and the working tempo target is **114 BPM**.

No full chorus lyric, full song lyric, exact melody, key, or final line distribution is canonical at this stage.

## Hook design requirements

Every route must:

- be concise enough to remember after one listen;
- feel natural when sung rather than reading like a thesis statement;
- remain emotionally legible without explaining algorithms, systems, or optimization directly;
- support the song title **HUMAN ERROR** without reducing it to a technology slogan;
- work in Chorus 1 while the production is still disciplined;
- gain emotional meaning in Chorus 2 as the arrangement becomes warmer and less controlled;
- survive a much larger Final Chorus with ensemble layers and EON's late upper-register escalation;
- leave room for the later lyric stage to ground the song in one concrete human situation;
- remain practical for Flow Music / Lyria generation.

The phrases below are hook nuclei and supporting anchors, not complete chorus lyrics.

## A — DON'T CORRECT ME

### Central hook nucleus

**“Call it human error / don’t correct me.”**

Possible supporting anchor:

**“I don’t wanna get it right.”**

### Hook logic

The emotional move is direct: the narrator initially recognizes that something is being judged as a mistake, then refuses correction. The phrase **“don’t correct me”** becomes more powerful every time the production becomes less controlled.

### Chorus-state behavior

- **Chorus 1:** “don’t correct me” can sound almost private or uncertain, with SOREN carrying a clean melodic line over disciplined production.
- **Chorus 2:** the same phrase becomes intentional rather than defensive; wider harmonies and human percussion make the refusal feel chosen.
- **Final Chorus:** the phrase can become a group-level declaration, with EON above the main line rather than replacing SOREN.
- **Error Tag:** a clipped or isolated fragment of “correct me” / “don’t” could work rhythmically later, but no final tag wording is locked now.

### Why it works

- Very strong connection to the bridge’s canonical reversal: stop trying to correct the error.
- Short, singable, easy to repeat.
- Lets the same lyric change meaning across the three chorus states.
- Uses the title naturally without requiring technological exposition.

### Main risk

“Don’t correct me” can drift into a broad self-acceptance anthem if the later lyric does not establish a specific choice, relationship, or human situation being judged as wrong.

## B — SAME MISTAKE TWICE

### Central hook nucleus

**“I’d make the same mistake twice.”**

Title anchor:

**“Call it human error.”**

Possible supporting idea:

**“At least the choice was mine.”**

### Hook logic

Instead of arguing abstractly that imperfection is human, the narrator proves it through a choice: knowing something may be considered a mistake and choosing it again anyway. This immediately turns the concept from theory into behavior.

### Chorus-state behavior

- **Chorus 1:** “I’d make the same mistake twice” can sound like a dangerous admission rather than a victory.
- **Chorus 2:** the repeated choice becomes conscious ownership; the groove can loosen beneath the same melodic phrase.
- **Final Chorus:** the line can become fully defiant, with group responses, rapper interjections, and EON counter-melody around the unchanged core phrase.
- **Outro potential:** the phrase can leave an emotionally ambiguous residue—was the choice wise or simply human? The song does not need to resolve that.

### Why it works

- Extremely memorable language with a built-in emotional story.
- Naturally pushes the later lyric toward one concrete recurring choice or attachment, solving HUMAN ERROR’s main abstraction risk.
- Feels human rather than technological.
- The title can function as a label placed on the choice instead of the entire lyric becoming about “errors.”
- Creates useful ambiguity: the listener can interpret the “mistake” romantically, personally, socially, or existentially.

### Main risk

If the later lyric defaults to generic romantic recklessness, the broader precision-versus-humanity idea can shrink into a conventional “I know you’re bad for me but I want you” song. The concrete scenario must remain specific without becoming cliché.

## C — LEAVE THE ERROR IN

### Central hook nucleus

**“Leave the error in.”**

Possible supporting anchor:

**“That’s the part that sounds like me.”**

Title anchor:

**“Human error.”**

### Hook logic

The metaphor treats a mistake like something that would normally be edited out of a recording, performance, image, or life. Instead of correcting it, the narrator asks for the imperfection to remain because it is the most recognizably human part.

### Chorus-state behavior

- **Chorus 1:** “leave the error in” can be delivered cleanly while the production still sounds heavily controlled, creating tension between lyric and sound.
- **Chorus 2:** audible imperfections, room, saturation, and wider vocal layers begin proving the phrase true.
- **Final Chorus:** the production itself becomes the answer; the song visibly “leaves the error in” through human-feeling percussion, live contamination, breaths, ad-libs, and layered voices.
- **Error Tag:** this route has the strongest natural relationship with the existing Error Tag section because the phrase can be fragmented rhythmically without needing a separate concept.

### Why it works

- Most distinctive phrase of the round.
- Strongest integration between lyric hook and production concept.
- Gives PRECISION BREAKS INTO BLOOD an unusually coherent identity.
- Can make the final chorus feel like the song has physically obeyed its own hook.

### Main risk

It is slightly more metaphorical and less immediately conversational than A or B. Without careful lyric grounding, listeners may interpret it mainly as a song about recording/performance rather than a human choice in ordinary life.

## D — PERFECT NEVER FELT ALIVE

### Central hook nucleus

**“Perfect never felt alive.”**

Title anchor:

**“Human error.”**

Possible supporting idea:

**“I don’t need to get it right.”**

### Hook logic

The phrase contrasts technical success with emotional emptiness. It does not defend mistakes directly; it argues that perfection itself failed to produce the feeling the narrator was supposed to want.

### Chorus-state behavior

- **Chorus 1:** SOREN can sing the phrase with controlled power while the production is still almost perfect, creating irony.
- **Chorus 2:** the arrangement becomes warmer as the lyric gains confidence.
- **Final Chorus:** “alive” becomes the natural place for EON’s late upper harmony, sustained counterline, or extension while SOREN retains the core melody.

### Why it works

- Very melodic and anthem-ready.
- Clear emotional meaning on first listen.
- Strong vowel shape for sustained singing, especially on “alive.”
- Naturally supports the late vocal escalation without forcing a separate high-note gimmick.

### Main risk

It is the least conceptually specific of the four and has the highest chance of sounding like a general empowerment lyric. “Alive” is also common pop vocabulary, so the surrounding lyric would need unusually concrete writing to keep HUMAN ERROR distinctive.

Choose **B — SAME MISTAKE TWICE** if the priority is the strongest complete hook foundation. It gives HUMAN ERROR a simple human behavior that listeners can understand immediately while preserving ambiguity for the later story.
