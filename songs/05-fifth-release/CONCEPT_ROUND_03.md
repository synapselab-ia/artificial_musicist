## Round intent

Round 01 and Round 02 were both rejected in full. No concept from either round was reserved.

Round 03 deliberately simplifies the method. Each option must work first as an immediately understandable human situation. The depth should come later from songwriting, point of view, arrangement, vocal contrast, and the final-state change — not from a complicated premise.

Do not reuse rejected Round 01 or Round 02 concepts. Do not automatically reuse the existing reserve concepts `DRESS REHEARSAL` or `NO SAINTS AFTER MIDNIGHT`.

## A — NOTHING HAPPENED

### Core concept

Two people crossed a line they had both been pretending did not exist. The next day, they agree without saying it to act as if **nothing happened**.

Everything ordinary now feels different.

### Emotional engine

`familiarity -> boundary crossed -> denial -> hyper-awareness -> failed normality -> admission`

### Why it could work

- extremely simple premise with immediate emotional tension;
- room for intimate verses and a much larger chorus without needing an elaborate story;
- the title can function differently each time: denial at first, accusation later, grief by the end;
- strong compatibility with UNFRAME's restraint-versus-release identity.

---

## B — ALMOST STRANGERS

### Core concept

Two people who once knew everything about each other meet again and realize they still know the old versions perfectly — but barely know who the other person is now.

They are not strangers. That is what makes it worse.

### Emotional engine

`recognition -> comfort -> mismatch -> nostalgia -> distance -> acceptance`

### Why it could work

- emotionally immediate without depending on a gimmick;
- gives the lyric concrete contrast between remembered details and present unfamiliarity;
- naturally supports a restrained opening and a devastating final section;
- mature relationship writing without repeating `Last Train Home` or `THE QUIET PART` directly.

---

## C — LEAVE THE LIGHT ON

### Core concept

Someone leaves after a rupture. The narrator refuses to chase them, beg, or promise that everything can be fixed.

But they leave one light on anyway.

### Emotional engine

`rupture -> pride -> silence -> waiting -> vulnerability -> unresolved invitation`

### Why it could work

- one simple physical image can carry the entire song;
- emotionally ambiguous: dignity and hope coexist;
- can be romantic, familial, or friendship-coded without changing the core;
- strong potential for a quiet final image rather than an over-explained resolution.

---

## D — DON'T MAKE ME THE BAD GUY

### Core concept

A relationship is ending and the other person needs the story to be simple: one person loved, one person failed, one person is the villain.

The narrator refuses to accept a version of the past that makes the breakup easier by making one person entirely guilty.

### Emotional engine

`breakup -> accusation -> defensiveness -> shared evidence -> exhaustion -> refusal of the simple story`

### Why it could work

- direct language and immediate hook potential;
- conflict without requiring manipulation, lies, or psychological-game mechanics;
- gives rap and vocal sections different evidentiary functions;
- can end without reconciliation while still preserving emotional complexity.

---

## E — EMPTY SEAT

### Core concept

A group keeps doing the same things after one person is no longer part of their life. Nobody talks about it directly, but every familiar place contains the shape of the missing person.

The seat is empty. Everyone notices.

### Emotional engine

`routine -> absence -> avoidance -> memory -> collective admission -> carrying on`

### Why it could work

- non-romantic by default, giving the catalogue new emotional territory;
- simple enough to become universal without becoming vague;
- naturally suited to a seven-member group through shared perspective and layered final vocals;
- strong visual hook without requiring death; the absence can come from estrangement, moving away, fallout, or any irreversible separation.
