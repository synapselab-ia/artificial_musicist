# LEAVE THE LIGHT ON — HOOK ROUND

Status: **OPEN — USER SELECTION REQUIRED**

Date: 2026-09-04

Locked concept: **LEAVE THE LIGHT ON**.
Locked sonic direction: **A — PORCHLIGHT PULSE**.
Locked architecture: **A — ONE WINDOW**.

## Hook design rules

- The hook must preserve dignity and hope simultaneously.
- It must not become a generic plea to come back.
- The title image should remain concrete and singable.
- Chorus language should be concise enough for clean Flow delivery.
- The final chorus must support a meaningful mutation rather than a simple louder repeat.
- The hook should sit naturally over alternative R&B + UK garage / 2-step movement around 118–122 BPM.

---

## A — I WON'T WAIT UP

**Recommended. Core contradiction: refusal to wait versus the deliberate light left on.**

Chorus nucleus:

> **I won't wait up**  
> **But I'll leave the light on**

Supporting direction:

The surrounding chorus can establish that the narrator will not call again, follow, or ask where the other person went. The title line then reveals the one exception.

Final mutation candidate:

> **I didn't wait up**  
> **But the light's still on**

Why it works:

- immediately understandable;
- strong conversational English;
- dignity and hope exist in the same two lines;
- the final mutation proves time passed without requiring reconciliation;
- leaves room for melody and production instead of overloading the chorus with text.

Primary risk:

`wait up` is colloquial and simple; the surrounding lyric must provide enough specificity so the hook feels emotionally distinctive rather than generic.

---

## B — ONE WINDOW

**Core image: the entire house goes dark except for one deliberate signal.**

Chorus nucleus:

> **Every room can go dark**  
> **I'll leave one window on**

Alternative title-anchor line later in the chorus:

> **Just enough to know I left the light on**

Final mutation candidate:

> **Every room went dark**  
> **One window stayed on**

Why it works:

- ties directly to the locked architecture name `ONE WINDOW`;
- creates a strong visual image;
- supports luminous production and late EON harmony naturally;
- final mutation can shift from intention to observed fact.

Primary risk:

`leave one window on` is slightly less idiomatic than `leave the light on`; wording would need careful lyric polishing to avoid sounding translated.

---

## C — YOU KNOW WHICH ONE

**Core idea: no explicit invitation is needed because the other person knows exactly which light belongs to the narrator.**

Chorus nucleus:

> **If you turn around**  
> **You know which one**  
> **I won't call you back**  
> **I'll leave the light on**

Final mutation candidate:

> **If you turned around**  
> **You knew which one**

Why it works:

- gives the light image personal history without explaining it;
- creates a strong melodic setup before the title line;
- feels intimate rather than pleading;
- provides room for a transformed past-tense final chorus.

Primary risk:

`If you turn around` can edge toward invitation/plea if the verses do not maintain the narrator's agency.

---

## D — NOT A PROMISE

**Core idea: explicitly reject the idea that leaving the light on means everything is forgiven or guaranteed.**

Chorus nucleus:

> **It's not a promise**  
> **It's not come home**  
> **I just can't make myself**  
> **Turn that light off**

Possible title resolution:

> **So I leave the light on**

Final mutation candidate:

> **It wasn't a promise**  
> **I still left the light on**

Why it works:

- most explicit articulation of the song's emotional ambiguity;
- prevents the concept from becoming a reconciliation fantasy;
- gives SOREN a chorus with clear emotional escalation;
- bridge can expose that the narrator's explanation is partly self-protection.

Primary risk:

More words and more explanation make it less instantly hooky than Route A.

---

## Recommendation

**A — I WON'T WAIT UP** is the strongest default hook route.

It compresses the entire song into one contradiction, is easy to understand on first listen, gives Flow clean short phrases, and supports a natural final mutation from intention to elapsed-time reality:

> **I won't wait up / But I'll leave the light on**

becoming

> **I didn't wait up / But the light's still on**

The final line preserves the unresolved ending: the narrator continued living through the night, but did not extinguish the invitation.
