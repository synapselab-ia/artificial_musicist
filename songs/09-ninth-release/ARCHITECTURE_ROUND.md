## A — FIRST BREATH

Dramatic principle: **the window opens early; the rest of the song is about learning to tolerate what comes through it**.

Architecture:

`CLOSED ROOM VERSE / NOEN -> PRE-CHORUS / ELIAN: HANDLE / CURTAIN / LIGHT -> CHORUS 1 / WINDOW OPEN -> OUTSIDE VERSE / SOREN + NOEN -> SHORT RHYTHMIC BLOCK / MIRO + ROOK + VALE -> PRE-CHORUS 2 / MORE AIR -> CHORUS 2 / WORLD TOO LOUD -> BRIDGE / I DON'T FEEL BETTER YET -> FINAL CHORUS / LEAVE IT OPEN -> EON COUNTERLINE -> BAND TAG`

### How it works

The physical action happens before the first full chorus. That gives the song time to explore the less obvious part of the concept: opening the window does not instantly feel good.

Verse 2 can contain concrete outside details — traffic, somebody laughing, wind lifting paper, a neighbor moving around — while the narrator remains slightly irritated and disconnected.

The single rhythmic block belongs in the middle, not at the beginning. MIRO / ROOK / VALE briefly inventory the closed-room habits that had become normal, then the melody takes back control.

The bridge strips the band down and states the emotional truth: the action was real even though recovery is incomplete.

### Strengths

- strongest literal narrative progression;
- gives the concept enough time after the window actually opens;
- clear room for all vocal functions;
- secondary rap feels purposeful rather than decorative;
- strong final mutation from `opened it` to `leave it open` territory.

### Risk

If Chorus 1 already feels too triumphant, the song can peak emotionally too soon. The first chorus must remain tentative.

## B — CURTAINS FIRST — SELECTED

Dramatic principle: **the song notices small changes before the narrator admits what he did**.

Canonical architecture:

`INTRO / GUITAR + ROOM TONE -> VERSE 1 / CLOSED ROUTINE -> PRE-CHORUS / SOMETHING MOVED -> CHORUS 1 / CURTAINS MOVING -> VERSE 2 / OUTSIDE SOUND ENTERS -> VOCAL LIFT / ELIAN + EON -> SHORT RHYTHMIC MIDDLE-EIGHT / CLOSED-HABIT INVENTORY -> CHORUS 2 / I OPENED IT -> BRIDGE / NOT BETTER YET -> FINAL CHORUS / LET THE ROOM CHANGE -> SOREN + EON LIFT -> QUIET GUITAR END`

### Canonical behavior

The first chorus does **not** explicitly reveal the whole act. It centers on sensory evidence: curtains moving, light changing, air crossing the room. The listener understands that something is different before the narrator names it.

Only later does the lyric plainly acknowledge that he opened the window.

The song must therefore remain cinematic and concrete rather than thesis-driven. Physical detail does narrative work before explanation does.

The rhythmic middle-eight remains short and compact — one contrast in cadence describing the routines that kept the room sealed — then rap/rhythmic delivery disappears as the melody takes back control.

The bridge must state incomplete recovery rather than victory. The final chorus then permits the room to keep changing instead of declaring the narrator fixed.

### Why selected

- most cinematic and least didactic;
- strongest fit for the live-band `CURTAINS MOVING` direction;
- excellent vocal-first pacing;
- lets guitars, harmonies, and sensory detail carry narrative information;
- keeps rap firmly secondary;
- creates a natural late reveal for the actual open-window action.

### Primary writing risk

The concept must remain understandable on first listen despite delaying explicit explanation. The hook therefore needs a strong sensory nucleus rather than an abstract slogan.

## C — SAME ROOM, MORE AIR

Dramatic principle: **the song keeps returning to almost the same chorus while the arrangement changes its meaning**.

Architecture:

`VERSE 1 / SEALED -> PRE-CHORUS -> CHORUS 1 / SMALL + NARROW -> VERSE 2 / WINDOW ACTION -> PRE-CHORUS 2 -> CHORUS 2 / SAME CORE HOOK, WIDER BAND -> SHORT RHYTHMIC CONTRAST / MIRO + ROOK -> BRIDGE / NOTHING IS FIXED -> FINAL CHORUS / SAME HOOK, FULL BAND + NEW LAST LINE -> EON COUNTERLINE -> OUTRO`

### How it works

The chorus nucleus stays nearly unchanged across the song while the room around it opens through arrangement.

### Risk

Relies heavily on Flow realizing meaningful arrangement growth between repeated choruses.
