## A — AIR IN THE ROOM

**Dream-pop vocal architecture + liquid breakbeat / light drum-and-bass motion + modern K-pop melodic layering.**

Working tempo: approximately **126–132 BPM**, but performed with spacious half-time vocal phrasing so it does not feel rushed.

### Core sound

- soft but detailed rolling breakbeat;
- warm sub-bass rather than aggressive 808s;
- glassy keys / blurred guitar textures;
- small environmental textures suggesting air, street movement, curtains, distant life without literal sound-effect gimmicks;
- choruses widen through stacked vocals rather than heavier drums.

### Vocal behavior

NOEN can begin almost dry and close, with little harmonic support. ELIAN introduces the first melodic lift. EON's upper harmonies arrive gradually, as if the room is literally gaining air. SOREN carries the largest chorus expansion, but the emotion stays open rather than belted into melodrama.

Rap appears once: a concise ROOK / MIRO rhythmic passage over the moving breakbeat, cataloguing the closed-room habits and sensory details. It should feel like momentum entering the song, not a rap showcase.

### Narrative-production arc

The instrumental starts muted and filtered. Each section reveals more top-end, stereo width, percussion detail, and vocal harmony. The final chorus uses nearly the same musical material as the first, but it is fully open and breathable.

### Strengths

- biggest sonic departure from recent UNFRAME songs while staying modern;
- vocal layering becomes the main event;
- movement without requiring a triumphant mood;
- excellent concept-to-production metaphor: the track literally gains air;
- rap can remain short but meaningful.

### Risk

At higher BPM, Flow may over-energize the song into something too euphoric. Prompting must protect half-time vocal phrasing and emotional restraint.

## B — CURTAINS MOVING

**Indie-pop / alternative rock + clean live-band dynamics + modern K-pop vocal production.**

Working tempo: approximately **112–118 BPM**.

### Core sound

- live-feeling drums with light room sound;
- warm electric bass;
- clean guitar picking in verses;
- brighter strummed / chiming guitars as the song opens;
- restrained synth support only for width and upper atmosphere;
- final chorus feels physically larger because the band opens up, not because the production becomes maximalist.

### Vocal behavior

This direction gives the vocal members the clearest traditional song space: NOEN intimate verse, ELIAN pre-chorus lift, SOREN broad melodic chorus, EON upper counterline.

VALE can contribute a low sung/rhythmic passage rather than a full rap verse. ROOK and MIRO share one concise rhythmic middle-eight or post-verse block, closer to conversational cadence than dense technical rap.

### Narrative-production arc

The first verse feels like a room with curtains still shut. Guitar and drums progressively gain presence. The bridge can briefly strip back to voice + guitar/bass pulse before the final chorus enters with the fullest natural-band sound.

### Strengths

- clearest genre departure in the catalog;
- strongest “real band” feeling;
- strong melodic chorus potential;
- naturally supports human-sounding vocals and breathing;
- rap can be secondary without feeling artificially inserted.

### Risk

Could drift into generic inspirational pop-rock if the lyric and harmonic language become too broad. The writing must stay concrete and slightly understated.

## C — OUTSIDE STILL MOVES

**Organic alternative pop + warm electronic percussion + acoustic guitar / piano details + layered contemporary K-pop vocals.**

Working tempo: approximately **100–106 BPM**.

### Core sound

- dry warm kick and soft percussion;
- acoustic guitar fragments and muted piano;
- subtle synth bass;
- small hand-percussion / shaker details entering as the outside world becomes more present;
- harmonies gradually accumulate rather than the instrumental becoming huge.

### Vocal behavior

The emotional center stays very close to the voice. NOEN can carry the most intimate material; ELIAN and EON widen the harmonic field; SOREN appears selectively for one major melodic payoff rather than dominating every chorus.

MIRO gets the natural secondary-rap role here: a short speech-to-song passage that lists the small routines of staying closed off. VALE and ROOK can contribute low support, rhythmic backing, or brief phrases instead of demanding full rap blocks.

### Narrative-production arc

The song begins almost domestic and tactile. The production adds small outside-world details section by section. Final chorus is not “bigger” in a stadium sense; it feels more populated, warmer, and less sealed.

### Strengths

- most emotionally natural interpretation;
- very strong vocal clarity and Flow robustness;
- restrained rap integrates easily;
- concrete sensory lyric can do most of the work;
- avoids both rap dominance and ballad cliché.

### Risk

Less radical sonically than A or B. Arrangement and hook would need enough personality to avoid feeling simply “nice.”

## D — STREETLIGHT MORNING

**Airy synth-pop + soft house pulse + atmospheric alt-pop / K-pop vocal architecture.**

Working tempo: approximately **116–122 BPM**.

### Core sound

- steady but soft four-on-the-floor pulse, never club-heavy;
- rounded synth bass;
- translucent pads and plucked synths;
- small guitar or piano touches for human texture;
- filtered first verse opening into brighter high frequencies and wider stereo space;
- subtle rhythmic lift rather than explosive drops.

### Vocal behavior

The song is predominantly sung. ELIAN and SOREN can shape the main melodic hooks, NOEN keeps the verses grounded, and EON supplies high-air harmony. The chorus should feel like taking a fuller breath, not like a dance-floor payoff.

Rap is extremely limited: one short VALE / MIRO rhythmic bridge or spoken-sung passage over the reduced pulse. ROOK can contribute precision through a brief rhythmic phrase rather than a technical verse.

### Narrative-production arc

The steady pulse represents life continuing outside the room even when the narrator is not participating. At first it feels distant; by the final chorus, the narrator is moving with it rather than hearing it through a wall.

### Strengths

- highly melodic and replayable;
- biggest shift toward polished pop without abandoning UNFRAME's emotional specificity;
- vocal harmonies can dominate;
- concept maps cleanly onto a constant pulse becoming welcomed rather than intrusive;
- rap naturally remains secondary.

### Risk

Too much brightness could make the emotional transition feel solved too early. Verses must remain muted and hesitant until the final third.

## Recommendation logic

- **A — AIR IN THE ROOM** if the priority is the most modern and texturally distinctive version: airy vocals over liquid rhythmic motion.
- **B — CURTAINS MOVING** if the priority is the biggest catalog departure while preserving a strong, human, vocal-driven UNFRAME identity.
- **C — OUTSIDE STILL MOVES** if the priority is emotional naturalism and maximum renderer safety.
- **D — STREETLIGHT MORNING** if the priority is polished melodic pop with the least rap emphasis and a subtle sense of forward motion.

## Recommendation

**B — CURTAINS MOVING** is the strongest default for the user's stated goal. It changes the instrumental language more radically than simply reducing the amount of rap: live-band dynamics, guitar-driven melodic growth, strong vocal space, and only a concise rhythmic contribution from the rap line. It can therefore sound genuinely new while the member functions and UNFRAME writing standard remain recognizable.
