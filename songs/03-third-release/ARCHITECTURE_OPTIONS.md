## Canonical architecture — TRANSLATION TABLE

### Core structural idea

The song begins as a normal, careful conversation. After the first refrain, the rap block acts like an annotated translation of what was just said. The second half returns to the same conversational language, but the listener can no longer hear it innocently. The bridge finally removes the need for translation by stating the truth directly.

### Canonical form

`INTRO / COLD OPEN -> VERSE 1 -> PRE-CHORUS -> REFRAIN 1 -> TRANSLATION RAP -> MIRO TURN -> VERSE 2 / SHORT BUILD -> REFRAIN 2 -> BRIDGE / CONFESSION -> FINAL AFTERMATH REFRAIN -> OUTRO`

- Outro: short unresolved decay or one final conversational residue.

### Bridge function

The bridge stops decoding euphemisms and delivers the sentence that makes the prior politeness obsolete. Bass and drums nearly disappear. This is the state change of the song.

### Final-section transformation

The refrain returns with altered meaning. Earlier, it asks for honesty or reacts to hidden meaning; afterward, it must respond to having actually received the truth. Harmony widens and the groove returns, but the mood must not become triumphant.

### Flow-facing rule

Keep member changes at section boundaries and preserve coherent vocal blocks. Do not convert the translation concept into rapid line-by-line singer switching.
