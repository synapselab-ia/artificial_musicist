# F7 — THIRD RELEASE CONCEPT ROUND

Status: **AWAITING USER SELECTION**

Date: 2026-09-04

## Design brief

This round begins the third UNFRAME release after the approved benchmarks `Last Train Home` and `HUMAN ERROR`.

The objective is not to repeat either benchmark's genre or story. Every option below is designed around:

- a distinct emotional and production territory;
- one concrete lyric scenario rather than abstract motivational writing;
- a concise, repeatable hook nucleus;
- meaningful rap/vocal contrast without seven line-by-line solo handoffs;
- a bridge that changes the song's perspective or stakes;
- a real final escalation and complete ending;
- authored/generation feasibility around **2:45–2:55**, with **3:00 as a hard Flow Music ceiling**.

Reserved concepts `DRESS REHEARSAL` and `NO SAINTS AFTER MIDNIGHT` are intentionally not reused in this round.

## A — MILE ZERO

### Core concept

A planned road trip fails almost immediately when the car breaks down before the group reaches the real destination. What begins as frustration turns into the night they remember most: convenience-store food, roadside jokes, headlights, an improvised speaker, and the realization that the destination was never the part that mattered.

### Concrete lyric scenario

The song starts with bags packed, a route loaded, and everyone expecting a perfect night somewhere else. The car dies. Plans collapse. Instead of calling the night ruined, they stay together and turn the dead stop into the event itself.

### Emotional engine

`expectation -> failure -> irritation -> improvisation -> collective release -> redefinition of success`

### Hook territory

**“We never made it past mile zero.”**

Secondary hook possibility: **“Maybe nowhere was the place to go.”**

### Sonic territory

Bright live-feeling pop-rock + punchy breakbeat + melodic hip-hop, with crunchy guitars, elastic bass, handclaps/stomps used selectively, and a chorus that feels communal rather than stadium-generic.

### Member-function potential

- VALE: dry low opening / grounded spoken-rap punctuation;
- ELIAN + NOEN: conversational first verse and melodic setup;
- ROOK: compact rhythmic rap built from failed-plan details;
- MIRO: speech-to-song transition that turns frustration into fun;
- SOREN: chorus ignition without oversinging the first refrain;
- EON: late upper harmony / final-chorus lift.

### Bridge purpose

The music strips down and the narrator briefly admits that the original destination mattered because they were trying to force a “perfect memory.” The final chorus returns with the realization that the imperfect stop became the real memory.

### Duration feasibility

**Strong.** Natural fit for a compact `V1 -> pre -> chorus -> rap/V2 -> chorus -> bridge -> final chorus -> short outro` structure around 2:48–2:53.

### Differentiation from benchmarks

Warmer, more communal, more physical/live, and more openly joyful than either approved release.

## B — GHOST MODE

### Core concept

For one night, the narrator deliberately disappears from the digital version of himself: phone off, location disabled, no read receipts, no posting, no algorithmic proof that the night happened. The song is not anti-technology; it is about the relief of being temporarily unreachable and unmeasured.

### Concrete lyric scenario

The opening is a stream of notifications, unanswered calls, typing bubbles, location sharing, and people expecting instant access. The narrator switches everything off and walks into the city with friends. By morning, nothing was posted—but the night feels more real because it was not converted into evidence.

### Emotional engine

`overexposure -> overload -> disappearance -> sensory presence -> freedom -> voluntary return`

### Hook territory

**“Tonight I’m going ghost mode.”**

Secondary hook possibility: **“If you can’t find me, I’m finally here.”**

### Sonic territory

Speed garage / liquid drum-and-bass influence + sleek K-pop topline + warm sub-bass, skittering percussion, clipped vocal fragments, and a chorus that opens melodically instead of becoming harder or more industrial.

### Member-function potential

- NOEN: intimate notification-overload opening;
- MIRO: rhythmic melodic block that transitions into motion;
- ROOK: syncopated rap using digital-language imagery without becoming gimmicky;
- VALE: low sparse answers / grounding contrast;
- ELIAN: pre-chorus lift;
- SOREN: clean melodic chorus lead;
- EON: airy upper layers and a floating final escalation.

### Bridge purpose

Everything drops to near-silence. The narrator realizes the goal is not to vanish forever: he wants to choose when he is available rather than live permanently accessible. The beat returns for a larger final chorus with human voices replacing some chopped digital textures.

### Duration feasibility

**Very strong.** Fast rhythmic movement allows a complete structure at approximately 2:45–2:50 without lyric rushing, provided the rap block stays compact.

### Differentiation from benchmarks

Fast and modern without HUMAN ERROR’s industrial severity; freer, brighter, and more kinetic, with a digital theme centered on presence rather than optimization/control.

## C — HALF-LIFE

### Core concept

A relationship is over, but pieces of it keep decaying slowly instead of disappearing: habits, phrases, routes, objects, and reflexes still carry traces of the other person. The narrator is not asking for reconciliation. He is frustrated that emotional residue lasts longer than the relationship itself.

### Concrete lyric scenario

The narrator has already deleted the obvious things and stopped contacting the person, yet keeps finding smaller residues: ordering two coffees by habit, avoiding one street, hearing a phrase he learned from them, reaching for a second key that is no longer there.

### Emotional engine

`separation -> false confidence -> residue -> irritation -> honest grief -> acceptance without reunion`

### Hook territory

**“You’re gone, but you still have a half-life.”**

Secondary hook possibility: **“Every day there’s less of you, never none.”**

### Sonic territory

Alternative R&B + trip-hop drums + dirty restrained guitar + low sub-bass, with a chorus that widens harmonically while keeping the groove heavy and intimate.

### Member-function potential

- NOEN: exposed first verse;
- VALE: low-response phrases / deep short rap;
- MIRO: melodic rap describing involuntary habits;
- ROOK: precise second rap pocket around “evidence” and residue;
- ELIAN: melodic pre-chorus connector;
- SOREN: controlled emotional chorus lead;
- EON: delayed upper counterline saved for final chorus.

### Bridge purpose

The bridge reveals the narrator has been treating recovery like a countdown. He stops asking when the other person will reach zero and accepts that healing is not mathematically clean.

### Duration feasibility

**Strong.** Best at approximately 2:50–2:55 with one combined rap/verse block rather than separate long rap sections.

### Differentiation from benchmarks

Emotionally serious like the debut, but more tactile, bitter, groove-heavy, and post-relationship rather than a live goodbye scene; sonically earthier and less cinematic.

## D — THE QUIET PART

### Core concept

A conversation is full of polished, socially acceptable sentences while the real meaning stays underneath. Each section exposes more of the subtext until the narrator finally says the thing everyone has been carefully avoiding.

### Concrete lyric scenario

Two people meet in a public place after something between them has clearly changed. They exchange safe lines—“you look good,” “work’s been busy,” “we should do this again”—while both know those sentences are evasions. The rap section translates the polite conversation into what each line actually means.

### Emotional engine

`politeness -> tension -> coded language -> exposure -> confession -> aftermath`

### Hook territory

**“Say the quiet part.”**

Secondary hook possibility: **“Don’t give me the clean version.”**

### Sonic territory

Neo-soul / minimal funk + crisp boom-bap-influenced drums + modern K-pop vocal layering, using negative space, bass guitar, muted keys, and occasional brass or guitar punctuation rather than a huge electronic wall.

### Member-function potential

- VALE: low conversational opening;
- NOEN: intimate subtext-heavy verse;
- ROOK: technical “translation” rap where polite lines are decoded;
- MIRO: half-sung response block that destabilizes the conversation;
- ELIAN: tension-building melodic pre-chorus;
- SOREN: restrained first chorus, larger confession later;
- EON: selective high harmony in the final reveal rather than constant ad-libs.

### Bridge purpose

The instrumental nearly stops and the narrator finally says the actual sentence that the entire song has been circling. The final chorus is no longer a demand to “say the quiet part”; it becomes the consequence of having said it.

### Duration feasibility

**Very strong.** The compact conversational architecture can land around 2:46–2:52 with excellent breathing room.

### Differentiation from benchmarks

More mature, groove-led, witty, and verbally precise; lower production density than HUMAN ERROR and less atmospheric/cinematic than Last Train Home.

## Creative read

- **MILE ZERO** is the strongest route if release three should reveal a warmer, communal, live-feeling side of UNFRAME.
- **GHOST MODE** is the strongest route if the goal is a very current, kinetic K-pop single with high replay value and a clean sonic break from both benchmarks.
- **HALF-LIFE** is the strongest route for emotional depth and a more mature post-breakup lyric without recreating `Last Train Home`.
- **THE QUIET PART** is the strongest route for songwriting craft, verbal tension, groove, and member-character performance.

No option is canonical until explicitly selected by the user.
