## A — VELVET KNIFE

### Sonic identity

**Neo-soul + minimal funk + modern K-pop vocal architecture.**

Warm electric bass, dry pocket drums, Rhodes-style keys, muted guitar punctuation, sparse hand percussion, and occasional restrained brass color. The groove should feel expensive and controlled rather than retro-costume funk.

### Tempo territory

Approximately **96–102 BPM**.

### Dynamic arc

`dry conversational opening -> bass-led verse -> restrained melodic lift -> wider but still controlled refrain -> rap translation pocket -> reduced second build -> near-silent bridge confession -> harmonically richer final refrain`

### Vocal behavior

- verses close-miked and conversational;
- first refrain intentionally restrained;
- ROOK's rap sits deep in the pocket rather than chasing maximum speed;
- MIRO can blur speech and melody after the rap;
- SOREN is held back early so the final section has real headroom;
- EON enters late as upper harmonic pressure rather than decorative ad-lib spam.

### Why it fits

This route puts the focus exactly where the concept is strongest: subtext, pauses, rhythm, and verbal precision. The instrumental can make polite sentences sound increasingly dangerous without becoming melodramatic.

### Risk

If generated too softly, it could become merely tasteful background R&B. The chorus/refrain still needs a memorable rhythmic/melodic identity and the final section must materially widen.

### 3-minute safety

**Excellent.** Moderate tempo and negative space allow articulate lyrics, a complete rap block, and a real bridge without rushing.

## B — POLITE DAMAGE

### Sonic identity

**Sleek alt-R&B + UK garage-informed drums + minimalist K-pop pop structure.**

Warm sub-bass, clipped two-step percussion, glassy keys, short guitar/synth stabs, subtle vocal chops used as punctuation, and a refrain that opens melodically over a more kinetic rhythm section.

### Tempo territory

Approximately **108–114 BPM**.

### Dynamic arc

`minimal pulse -> syncopated verse -> tension-building pre -> open melodic refrain -> tighter rap/garage pocket -> second refrain with extra counter-rhythm -> beat collapse for confession -> full two-step return with stacked vocals`

### Vocal behavior

- NOEN and VALE can make the opening feel private despite the faster beat;
- ELIAN provides clean upward tension into the refrain;
- ROOK gets the sharpest rhythmic section;
- MIRO bridges rap into melodic language naturally;
- SOREN carries a clean, memorable main refrain rather than a huge belt from the start;
- EON's late layers make the final return feel airborne.

### Why it fits

The polished social surface of the lyric contrasts well with a sleek, controlled beat underneath. As the truth leaks out, rhythmic details can become less tidy and more human without repeating HUMAN ERROR's industrial language.

### Risk

The digital/modern production must remain subordinate to the conversation. Too many vocal chops or synthetic details would pull attention away from the lyric and drift toward a generic trendy K-pop track.

### 3-minute safety

**Excellent.** The faster rhythmic engine supports a concise structure around 2:46–2:50 while preserving bridge space.

## C — SMILE LIKE NOTHING

### Sonic identity

**Contemporary funk-pop + boom-bap backbone + subtle new-jack-swing energy.**

Rubbery bass guitar, crisp snare, swung percussion accents, dry rhythm guitar, compact synth brass, and selective group responses. More physical and performance-forward than the other routes, but still built around lyrical tension rather than party energy.

### Tempo territory

Approximately **104–110 BPM**.

### Dynamic arc

`polished groove -> call-and-response verse -> sharper pre-chorus -> hooky rhythmic refrain -> boom-bap translation rap -> groove interruption -> exposed confession -> final refrain with group responses turning uncomfortable instead of celebratory`

### Vocal behavior

- VALE can open with dry conversational authority;
- NOEN plays against the upbeat groove with emotionally restrained lines;
- ROOK's rap functions almost like an annotated transcript of the conversation;
- MIRO can echo or correct phrases melodically;
- SOREN gives the refrain punch without turning it into a power ballad;
- EON adds selective high responses only in the final escalation.

### Why it fits

The central irony becomes musical: everyone sounds smooth and socially composed while the lyric is increasingly uncomfortable. That mismatch can make the hook especially memorable and stage-friendly.

### Risk

If the groove becomes too cheerful, the emotional stakes may feel trivial. The production needs a slightly tense harmony/bass movement and a bridge that clearly breaks the social mask.

### 3-minute safety

**Very strong.** Compact grooves and a relatively direct form can land around 2:47–2:52 without compression.

## D — NO CLEAN VERSION

### Sonic identity

**Minimal hip-hop / alt-R&B + neo-soul harmony + extreme negative space.**

Deep sub-bass, rimshots/clicks, sparse piano or detuned electric keys, occasional low guitar texture, room-tone-like ambience, and very little continuous instrumentation. Silence and interruption become compositional tools.

### Tempo territory

Approximately **88–96 BPM**.

### Dynamic arc

`almost-empty conversation -> low groove enters -> melodic pressure builds without density -> refrain with wide harmony but sparse drums -> technical rap over stripped percussion -> second refrain with more vocal overlap -> bridge drops nearly to silence -> final section rebuilds from voices first, instruments second`

### Vocal behavior

- strongest route for VALE and NOEN's intimate low-pressure colors;
- ROOK's articulation becomes extremely exposed, so the rap must be precise rather than maximal-speed;
- MIRO can function as the emotional hinge between spoken cadence and melody;
- SOREN's restraint is crucial until the late release;
- EON's first truly bright upper layer should arrive only after the confession.

### Why it fits

This route turns the unsaid material into literal sonic space. Every pause can feel like a sentence someone chose not to finish, making the bridge confession especially powerful.

### Risk

It is the least forgiving option. Weak melody, vague lyrics, or generic generation choices would be exposed immediately. It also needs enough rhythmic identity to avoid becoming slow and static.

### 3-minute safety

**Strong.** The low tempo does not require a long song if verses remain concise; however, over-writing would be dangerous because conversational phrasing consumes real time.
