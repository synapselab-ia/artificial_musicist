## Locked concept

**ZERO SUM**

A social environment turns attention, money, status, opportunities, praise, beauty, numbers, and even friendship into one shared scoreboard. The narrator is initially seduced by being ahead, then recognizes that a system requiring everyone else to become smaller is not a meaningful victory.

Emotional engine:

`comparison -> competition -> temporary high -> escalation -> disgust -> refusal of the scoreboard -> self-defined win`

## Broad reference abstraction

The user requested a rap-forward direction informed by a study of Agust D's catalogue.

Transfer only broad traits:

- rap as the center of gravity;
- sparse-but-heavy production;
- forceful drums and low end with room for diction;
- delivery-mode changes as structural escalation;
- short/simple hooks contrasting dense verses;
- aggression tied to an actual argument;
- an exposed emotional state-change section;
- genre contrast without copying any specific recording.

Do not reproduce recognizable melodies, flows, traditional-instrument signatures, lyrical devices, or arrangements from Agust D songs.

## Direction A — SCOREBOARD TEETH

**Minimalist hard hip-hop + modern trap + abrasive K-pop vocal architecture.**

Working tempo: approximately **98–102 BPM**, with selective double-time rap pockets rather than constant speed.

Core sound:

- dry, chest-forward kick and snare;
- deep controlled sub-bass;
- one short, hostile repeating synth/plucked motif;
- tiny digital clicks, count-ins, score ticks, and muted impact sounds used as scoreboard punctuation;
- very little pad density during main rap sections;
- chorus gains width through vocals and bass movement rather than a huge EDM stack.

Dramatic behavior:

The first half makes competition feel addictive. ROOK's central technical rap becomes increasingly dense as the categories being measured multiply. MIRO then changes the pocket instead of merely continuing the same flow. The bridge strips the beat almost entirely and exposes the realization that the narrator no longer knows whether he actually wants the things he is trying to win.

Final section:

The scoreboard sounds begin to disappear. The final chorus is bigger vocally but **less mechanically busy**, sonically demonstrating that self-defined value no longer needs constant measurement.

Why choose it:

- strongest direct fit for the requested rap-first reference abstraction;
- maximizes diction and cadence contrast;
- gives Flow less incentive to compress the lyric;
- keeps `ZERO SUM` distinct from `HUMAN ERROR` by avoiding industrial-machine production as the main identity.

Primary risk:

If generated too generically, the sparse trap base could feel underwritten. The motif, percussion punctuation, hook rhythm, and vocal architecture would need unusually strong identities.

## Direction B — DEAD HEAT

**Shadowy drill-influenced hip-hop + sub-heavy minimalism + tense melodic K-pop.**

Working feel: approximately **72–76 BPM half-time / 144–152 BPM rhythmic grid**, but authored to feel spacious rather than frantic.

Core sound:

- sliding low bass;
- clipped drill-adjacent hi-hat figures;
- dark piano fragments and low synthetic brass pressure;
- snare placement that creates a stalking rather than celebratory groove;
- sparse atmosphere around the rap;
- melodic hooks delivered with controlled tension instead of bright pop lift.

Dramatic behavior:

Competition feels paranoid rather than glamorous: every gain is immediately threatened by someone else's next move. ROOK can attack the grid in compact bursts while VALE and MIRO create slower, lower-register counters. Chorus sections widen harmonically but remain ominous.

Bridge:

A near-whispered exposed block interrupts the constant threat response before the final section rejects the race entirely.

Why choose it:

- darkest and most menacing option;
- strong flow-switch possibilities;
- gives the seven-member vocal palette a very different environment from existing releases.

Primary risk:

Drill signifiers can become stylistically generic if overused, and Flow may interpret the higher rhythmic grid as an excuse to rush delivery. Prompting would need to explicitly protect intelligibility.

## Direction C — TROPHY DUST

**Grimy sample-minded hip-hop + boom-bap backbone + modern sub-bass + contemporary K-pop hook design.**

Working tempo: approximately **92–96 BPM**.

Core sound:

- dusty drum texture with a modern low-end extension;
- chopped, original soul/guitar/keyboard-like micro-samples created as texture rather than recognizable sampling;
- scratches/noise fragments used sparingly;
- heavier snare pocket and less electronic gloss;
- chorus uses warm layered vocals against a deliberately rough beat.

Dramatic behavior:

The competition initially sounds triumphant and charismatic, like a victory lap. As the narrative progresses, the same loop starts feeling claustrophobic because the narrator cannot stop counting. Rap can be dense without relying on double-time because the pocket itself carries authority.

Bridge:

The loop drops out and leaves a more human harmonic bed, after which the final section returns with altered drums and no victory-lap posture.

Why choose it:

- strongest connection to classic/mixtape hip-hop energy rather than modern K-pop polish;
- lets wordplay, swagger, and storytelling carry the song;
- could become one of UNFRAME's most clearly hip-hop-centered singles.

Primary risk:

It may feel less immediately 'K-pop single' unless melodic architecture and final escalation are handled carefully.

## Direction D — NO PODIUM

**Dark alternative hip-hop + distorted rock abrasion + trap percussion + dramatic vocal release.**

Working tempo: approximately **96–100 BPM**.

Core sound:

- murky distorted guitar/riff fragments used rhythmically, not as a full rock wall;
- clipping or overdriven drum texture at selected peaks;
- thick sub under otherwise narrow verses;
- raw vocal doubles and rough-edged transitions;
- chorus opens into more melodic harmony without becoming polished arena rock.

Dramatic behavior:

The music begins controlled, but competitive obsession progressively contaminates the production. Rap delivery grows harsher as the narrator starts behaving like the people he despises. The bridge becomes the emotional rupture rather than another escalation.

Final section:

Instead of a bigger distortion pile, the track comes back clearer and more deliberate, making the rejection of the scoreboard feel like regaining hearing after noise.

Why choose it:

- most emotionally volatile option;
- creates a strong aggression/vulnerability contrast;
- provides substantial roles for the melodic members without weakening the rap center.

Primary risk:

Too much guitar/distortion could pull the track toward rap-rock and reduce the precise, lean character requested from the reference analysis.

## Recommendation

**A — SCOREBOARD TEETH** is the strongest fit.

It captures the most useful high-level lessons from the requested reference—space, attack, cadence changes, heavy low end, simple reset hooks, and a vulnerable interruption—while remaining clearly original to UNFRAME and specifically tied to the `ZERO SUM` concept.
