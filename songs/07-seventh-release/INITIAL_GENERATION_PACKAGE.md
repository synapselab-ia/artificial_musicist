The concept is specific: the speaker lives inside a social scoreboard where attention, money, praise, appearance, opportunities, invitations, status, numbers, and even friendship are treated as limited resources. At first, being ahead feels good. Then every victory creates another category to lose, and the speaker realizes he has started needing other people to become smaller just to feel valuable. In the bridge, strip almost all percussion and leave the vocal exposed enough for “What do I actually want” to land as the irreversible state change. When the final chorus returns, make it vocally larger but mechanically simpler: remove most scoreboard punctuation so the production itself stops measuring everything.

Use coherent section-level male vocal blocks rather than rapid line-by-line singer switching. Do not make the ending generically inspirational or overly sentimental; keep it controlled, confident, and earned.
