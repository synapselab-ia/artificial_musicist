## Concept / semantic audit

The song remains centered on manufactured scarcity and comparative value rather than drifting into perfectionism, persona, or misrepresentation.

The progression is concrete:

- VALE introduces ranking behavior through seats, names, and visible priority;
- Verse 1 establishes comparison as a learned mental accounting system;
- Chorus 1 states the zero-sum rule as something the narrator currently believes;
- MIRO expands the ranking system into access, appearance, invitations, praise, and ownership desire;
- ROOK converts life, friendship, opportunity, and attention into measurable categories and exposes the self-renewing nature of the competition;
- MIRO's pocket break is the first direct interruption of the logic;
- Chorus 2 shows that first place creates another metric instead of satisfaction;
- NOEN's bridge removes the scoreboard and asks what the narrator wants without somebody beneath him;
- the final chorus rejects the assumption that another person's gain subtracts from the narrator.

Emotional motion remains:

`comparison -> competition -> temporary high -> escalation -> disgust -> refusal of the scoreboard -> self-defined win`.

## English-language audit

The lyric reads as contemporary native-style English rather than literal translation. Rap compression is intentional and remains semantically legible.

Key lines are grammatically and idiomatically natural, including:

- `I learned to read the room like a balance sheet`;
- `Every rung felt better with somebody under you`;
- `When did your good news turn into my bad day?`;
- `Maybe I confused being ahead / With having somewhere to be`;
- `You go up, I don't go down / It was never zero sum`.

## Hook audit

Locked early-state nucleus:

`One up, one down / That's the only way we count.`

Locked final mutation:

`You go up, I don't go down / It was never zero sum.`

The mutation is genuine rather than cosmetic: the early chorus treats value as relative and subtractive; the final chorus rejects that premise.

## Rap audit

The track is genuinely rap-forward.

- VALE carries a substantial low-register opening and first rap block rather than a token intro;
- MIRO owns the category-expansion verse and later changes function into a slower pocket break;
- ROOK receives the largest and densest technical ascent, with internal escalation from metrics to relationships to self-recognition;
- SOREN's choruses remain intentionally short so melodic sections reset the listener rather than compete with the rap density;
- NOEN's exposed bridge supplies the required vulnerability/state change.

The ROOK block supports selective double-time, but the generation prompt must explicitly prohibit rushing the entire section merely to fit duration.

## Seven-member function audit

All seven canonical functions remain meaningful:

- M01 VALE — cold authority, first deep-rap block, later low realization;
- M02 SOREN — stable main chorus identity across belief and rejection states;
- M03 NOEN — exposed bridge and central self-questioning;
- M04 ROOK — technical rap centerpiece / maximum escalation;
- M05 ELIAN — concise melodic pre-hook lift;
- M06 MIRO — category-expansion rap plus contrasting pocket break;
- M07 EON — delayed high-register final expansion.

Flow-facing orchestration should preserve these as section-level vocal archetypes rather than fragile line-by-line voice identities.

## Production / lyric interaction audit

`SCOREBOARD TEETH` production devices remain narratively useful:

- score ticks/count punctuation can appear around the cold open and early chorus states;
- rap blocks should stay dry and relatively sparse for articulation;
- score punctuation may intensify through ROOK's ascent;
- MIRO's `Hold up` should create an audible pocket interruption;
- the bridge should strip almost all percussion;
- the final chorus should return larger vocally but with fewer mechanical score sounds;
- no active lead line should be chopped or obscured for effect.

## Duration audit

The draft contains approximately **446 lexical words**, comparable to the approved `DON'T QUOTE ME` lyric but at a slower working tempo of approximately 98–102 BPM.

The duration plan remains viable because:

- both main choruses are very short;
- the pre-hook is compact;
- transitions are economical;
- the dense language is concentrated primarily in rap blocks;
- only ROOK's ascent should use selective double-time;
- there is no post-chorus, dance break, instrumental solo, or long outro.

Primary risk: Flow may try to accelerate the technical ascent or compress the bridge/final chorus. Mitigation must be explicit in the generation prompt: protect diction and the complete final state, and use the requested 2:55–2:58 window for breathing/section completion rather than added repetitions.
