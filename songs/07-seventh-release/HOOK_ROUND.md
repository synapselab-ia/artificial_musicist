# F11 — ZERO SUM Hook Round

Status: **OPEN — USER SELECTION REQUIRED**

Opened: 2026-09-05

## Locked inputs

- Concept: **ZERO SUM**.
- Sonic direction: **SCOREBOARD TEETH**.
- Architecture: **THE LADDER**.
- Rap is the structural center; the hook must be compact enough to reset the ear between dense rap sections.
- The hook should support a clear final mutation after the `WHAT AM I WINNING?` bridge.
- English must remain natural, direct, and easy for Flow to articulate.

## Hook A — ONE UP / ONE DOWN

Core nucleus:

**“One up, one down — that’s the only way we count.”**

Title anchor:

**“Zero sum.”**

Final mutation:

**“You go up, I don’t go down — it was never zero sum.”**

Why it works:

- immediately communicates the competitive rule;
- short, chantable, and strong against a sparse hard-hip-hop beat;
- gives SOREN a melodic but rhythmically forceful chorus without softening the song;
- final mutation states the conceptual breakthrough in one clean reversal;
- fits `THE LADDER` because every earlier section is about climbing relative to somebody else.

Primary risk:

Needs strong melodic contour and rhythmic phrasing so `one up / one down` does not become too nursery-rhyme simple.

## Hook B — KEEP THE SCORE

Core nucleus:

**“Keep the score / keep the score / tell me what I’m worth in numbers.”**

Final mutation:

**“Lose the score / I know what I’m worth without it.”**

Why it works:

- turns the scoreboard production device directly into the chorus;
- highly repeatable and easy to understand;
- creates a clean production opportunity for score ticks and count punctuation;
- final mutation naturally supports the disappearance of mechanical counting sounds.

Primary risk:

More explicit and less psychologically sharp than A; it explains the concept rather than embodying the zero-sum logic.

## Hook C — FIRST PLACE / EMPTY ROOM

Core nucleus:

**“First place, empty room — tell me what that proves.”**

Supporting idea:

The narrator gets exactly what the ranking system promised and discovers that winning only works if someone remains present to lose.

Final mutation:

**“No place, no podium — I’ve got nothing left to prove.”**

Why it works:

- strongest image-based option;
- adds emotional loneliness beneath the aggression;
- naturally sets up the bridge realization;
- gives the final chorus a large emotional release without becoming inspirational-pop generic.

Primary risk:

Moves the hook slightly away from the literal `ZERO SUM` title, so the title would need a strong secondary placement elsewhere.

## Hook D — MORE

Core nucleus:

**“One more rung / one more name below me / one more, one more.”**

Final mutation:

**“No more rung / no more names below me / enough is enough.”**

Why it works:

- captures the addictive escalation of `THE LADDER`;
- repetition can become hypnotic and increasingly hostile;
- excellent contrast against ROOK’s dense technical rap;
- lets the final chorus replace compulsive accumulation with a deliberate stop.

Primary risk:

`More` is a very broad hook word and could feel generic unless the surrounding lyric keeps the scoreboard imagery extremely concrete.

## Recommendation

**A — ONE UP / ONE DOWN** is the strongest route.

It is the cleanest fusion of title, concept, sparse rap-first production, `THE LADDER` architecture, and transformed final state. The central reversal — **“You go up, I don’t go down”** — expresses the entire thesis without turning the chorus into a lecture.
