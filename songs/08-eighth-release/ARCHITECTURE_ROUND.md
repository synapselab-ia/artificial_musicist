## A — PASS THE MIC

Dramatic principle: **every answer creates the next answer**.

Architecture:

`COLD OPEN / NOEN STARTS -> VALE ANSWER -> ROOK REBUTTAL -> MIRO THIRD OPTION -> ELIAN LIFT -> CHORUS 1 / ANSWERS ANSWERS -> NOEN TRIES AGAIN -> SOREN HARDER ANSWER -> ROOK / VALE COUNTER-ROUND -> MIRO ESCALATION -> CHORUS 2 / EVERYBODY KNOWS -> BRIDGE / ASK ME -> LISTENING RESPONSE -> FINAL CHORUS / QUESTIONS NOT COMMANDS -> EON TAG`

### How it works

NOEN begins the problem but does not finish. VALE gives a blunt practical answer. ROOK immediately explains why that answer is incomplete. MIRO proposes a conditional third route. ELIAN tries to smooth the tension melodically.

The first chorus converts their responses into a rhythmic repeated premise: everybody has an answer.

NOEN tries to speak again, but SOREN enters with the most emotionally forceful advice yet, triggering a second argumentative round. The pressure peaks before the bridge.

After **“Can one of you ask me what I want?”**, the exact same people who previously issued commands begin asking short questions and leaving space.

### Strengths

- clearest conversational causality;
- easiest to understand who is responding to whom;
- excellent Flow robustness;
- gives all seven meaningful functions without equal-length solos;
- naturally demonstrates changed behavior in the final section.

### Risk

Can feel slightly orderly if the lyric does not include enough interruption and friction inside the blocks.

## B — PILE-ON

Dramatic principle: **help becomes pressure through accumulation**.

Architecture:

`NOEN HALF-CONFESSION -> VALE SOLUTION -> ROOK COUNTER-SOLUTION -> SHORT REFRAIN -> MIRO QUALIFIES BOTH -> ELIAN SUPPORT -> SOREN SUPPORT / OVERRIDE -> CHORUS 1 / DO THIS DO THAT -> ROOK RAP / WHY WAIT -> VALE RAP / WHY RUSH -> MIRO SPLIT DECISION -> CHORUS 2 / ADVICE PILE-UP -> BREAK / NOEN: ASK ME -> EMPTY BAR -> NOEN ANSWER -> FINAL CHORUS / ONE QUESTION AT A TIME -> EON + GROUP TAG`

### How it works

The song does not wait until Verse 2 to become crowded. Every new speaker adds another solution to the previous one.

A short refrain appears early and becomes increasingly overloaded each time it returns. By Chorus 2, pieces of earlier advice can appear as controlled group reactions around the lead rather than seven simultaneous full lines.

VALE and ROOK receive opposing substantial rap blocks in the second half, with MIRO refusing the binary and making the argument even more complicated.

At maximum density, everything cuts out for NOEN.

After the bridge, NOEN finally gets an uninterrupted answer block before the final chorus.

### Strengths

- strongest dramatic escalation;
- best match for TALKBACK's stop-start production;
- makes the listener experience why the advice becomes suffocating;
- strongest rap payoff for VALE / ROOK / MIRO;
- the bridge silence has maximum contrast.

### Risk

Highest lyric-density pressure. The second half must be written conservatively so Flow does not compress or omit lines near 3:00.

## C — SIDES OF THE TABLE

Dramatic principle: **the six advisers form opposing camps while the person with the problem disappears between them**.

Architecture:

`NOEN PROBLEM -> CAMP A / VALE + ELIAN -> CAMP B / ROOK + SOREN -> MIRO MEDIATES -> CHORUS 1 / STAY OR GO -> NOEN INTERRUPTED -> CAMP A RAP / CASE FOR ACTION -> CAMP B RAP / CASE FOR PATIENCE -> EON COUNTERLINE -> CHORUS 2 / BOTH SIDES LOUDER -> BRIDGE / YOU'RE BOTH TALKING ABOUT ME -> ASK ME -> FINAL / NO CAMPS -> GROUP LISTENING TAG`

### How it works

Rather than six independent pieces of advice, the members organically cluster around two broad answers.

VALE and ELIAN may represent decisive action; ROOK and SOREN may defend a more cautious or confrontational alternative depending on the eventual concrete scenario. MIRO tries to reconcile them, but his mediation becomes another form of speaking for NOEN.

EON arrives late with a counterline that exposes how completely the argument has become about the advisers rather than the original speaker.

The bridge dissolves both camps.

### Strengths

- easiest seven-member staging and choreography;
- creates real call-and-response between factions;
- allows strong sung/rap contrast;
- lower micro-switching risk than literal six-way advice;
- visually memorable.

### Risk

Could make the song feel like a debate between two sides instead of the more natural messy conversation originally requested.

## D — WRONG QUESTION

Dramatic principle: **everyone keeps answering a question NOEN never asked**.

Architecture:

`COLD OPEN / NOEN: I DON'T KNOW WHAT TO DO -> VALE ANSWERS -> ROOK CORRECTS -> CHORUS 1 / HERE'S WHAT YOU DO -> MIRO: THAT'S NOT WHAT HE SAID -> ELIAN / SOREN ADVICE LOOP -> ROOK TECHNICAL RAP -> NOEN ATTEMPT / INTERRUPTED -> CHORUS 2 / HERE'S WHAT YOU DO (MAXIMUM) -> BRIDGE / THAT WASN'T MY QUESTION -> CAN ONE OF YOU ASK ME WHAT I WANT? -> SILENCE -> NOEN ACTUAL WANT -> FINAL CHORUS / WHAT DO YOU WANT? -> EON TAG`

### How it works

This architecture builds around a very clear recurring chorus mechanism:

**“Here’s what you do…”**

Every adviser thinks they are answering the problem correctly, but MIRO becomes the first member to notice that they may be answering a different question from the one NOEN is trying to express.

The bridge reveals the structural error explicitly: nobody asked what outcome he wanted.

The final chorus mutates the commanding refrain from **“Here’s what you do”** into a question-led response such as **“What do you want?”**

### Strengths

- strongest hook architecture;
- extremely clear final mutation;
- easy for listeners to understand on first play;
- conversation remains central without requiring chaotic overlap;
- excellent balance of rap structure and melodic payoff.

### Risk

The thesis is more explicit than the other options; lyrics must avoid sounding didactic or like a lesson.
