# F12 — NO ONE ASKED Hook Round

Status: **OPEN — USER SELECTION REQUIRED**

Opened: 2026-09-05

## Locked inputs

Concept: **NO ONE ASKED**.

Sonic direction: **B — TALKBACK**.

Architecture: **D — WRONG QUESTION**.

Core hook requirement:

The early hook must embody the advisers' certainty and the final hook must embody listening. The mutation should feel musically inevitable but narratively different.

Mandatory bridge state change:

**“That wasn't my question.”**

**“Can one of you ask me what I want?”**

The hook must stay concise enough for a rap-forward song under the 3:00 Flow ceiling and must not sound like a communication lesson.

---

## A — HERE'S WHAT YOU DO

### Early-state nucleus

**“Here’s what you do—”**

The phrase becomes a launchpad for conflicting answers. Different members can complete it with contrasting commands while the chorus keeps returning to the same opening certainty.

Possible chorus behavior:

**“Here’s what you do— / don’t wait, don’t call / Here’s what you do— / no, forget that, walk.”**

The exact lyric is not locked here; the mechanism is.

### Final mutation

**“What do you want?”**

Instead of completing a command, the group leaves a gap after the question for NOEN to answer.

### Strengths

- most faithful to `WRONG QUESTION`;
- extremely clear conversational transformation;
- easy call-and-response;
- strong stop-start production opportunities;
- very Flow-friendly.

### Risk

The phrase is deliberately plain. The surrounding lyric must give it personality so it does not sound generic.

---

## B — IF I WERE YOU

### Early-state nucleus

**“If I were you…”**

Every adviser uses the same phrase to justify a different solution. The repetition makes the hidden problem obvious: everybody is projecting themselves into NOEN's place.

Potential rhythmic behavior:

**“If I were you, I’d leave / If I were you, I’d stay / If I were you— / yeah, that’s easy for you to say.”**

Again, this is hook territory, not locked final lyric.

### Final mutation

**“But you’re not me.”**

Then the group finally asks:

**“So what do you want?”**

### Strengths

- strongest natural spoken-language hook;
- inherently conversational;
- gives multiple members believable entry points;
- title concept becomes emotionally clear without saying `NO ONE ASKED` every time;
- excellent rap-to-vocal transition potential.

### Risk

`But you're not me` can sound confrontational if delivered too triumphantly. The final state must feel like restored agency, not rejection of the group.

---

## C — YOU SHOULD

### Early-state nucleus

**“You should—”**

The chorus becomes a rhythmic collision of advice:

**“You should leave / you should wait / you should call / you should—”**

The phrase can be chopped by beat dropouts and rebuttals, making the syntax itself feel interrupted.

### Final mutation

**“Should I?”**

The certainty collapses into a question. After the bridge, the group no longer finishes NOEN's sentence for him.

A final answer state may pivot into:

**“What do you want?”**

### Strengths

- most percussive / rap-friendly hook;
- very compact lyric budget;
- strong crowd-response potential;
- makes TALKBACK's clipped punctuation part of the hook itself.

### Risk

Could become too slogan-like or repetitive if the individual advice lines are not concrete enough.

---

## D — NO ONE ASKED

### Early-state nucleus

**“No one asked / everybody answered.”**

This makes the title itself the central hook and summarizes what the listener is hearing without requiring abstract explanation.

The first chorus can contrast NOEN's lack of room with the advisers' certainty:

**“No one asked / everybody answered / I barely said it / you already had the answer.”**

This wording is provisional hook territory, not approved lyric.

### Final mutation

**“Now you ask / I can answer.”**

The change is small but structural: the same group that filled the silence now creates it deliberately.

A final response can leave space around:

**“What do you want?”**

### Strengths

- strongest title integration;
- instantly communicates the premise;
- memorable antithesis: `asked / answered`;
- final mutation naturally demonstrates changed behavior;
- concise and easy to recognize on first listen.

### Risk

Because it states the concept very cleanly, the verses must remain conversational and messy enough that the song does not feel over-explained.
