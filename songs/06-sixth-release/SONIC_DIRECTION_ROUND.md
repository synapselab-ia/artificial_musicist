# SIXTH RELEASE — SONIC DIRECTION ROUND

Status: **OPEN — AWAITING USER SELECTION**

Date: 2026-09-05

Selected concept: **DON'T QUOTE ME**

## Design objective

Build a rap-forward UNFRAME track where fragmentation, interruption, quotation, and restored context are audible in the arrangement itself.

`HUMAN ERROR` remains the benchmark for momentum, rap integration, member contrast, and group functionality, but this release must not reuse its industrial-electro identity.

Mandatory constraints:

- rap is a structural backbone, not a single isolated verse;
- all seven members have meaningful musical functions;
- VALE, ROOK, and MIRO must remain rhythmically and tonally distinct;
- SOREN, NOEN, ELIAN, and EON retain real melodic/dramatic jobs;
- Flow-facing orchestration must rely on coherent section-level blocks rather than rapid member-by-member line switching;
- arrangement must support the concept's movement from clipped/distorted fragments to one complete uncontested statement;
- no sonic option below is canon until the user selects it.

## A — RECEIPT TAPE

**Contemporary hip-hop + electro-funk + chopped vocal-texture K-pop**

Working tempo: approximately **118–122 BPM**.

Core sound:

- dry, punchy drums with a hard pocket rather than a huge wall of percussion;
- elastic sub-bass and short funk-bass accents;
- original chopped vocal fragments used like corrupted quotations;
- clipped synth stabs, tape-stop gestures, mutes, reverses, and sudden dropouts;
- minimal harmony under rap so diction remains dominant;
- chorus opens wider with real melody and stacked group support, but stays rhythmically driven.

Dramatic behavior:

The track begins as if pieces of a statement are being cut up and rearranged. ROOK gets the most technically dense rap block; VALE answers with slower low-register authority; MIRO converts the fragmented rhythm into a melodic-rap bridge. Vocal sections progressively restore continuity until the final chorus delivers the full statement with no cuts.

Seven-member function:

- VALE: low opening / deep rebuttal rap;
- ROOK: primary technical dissection block;
- MIRO: melodic-rap reconstruction;
- NOEN: exposed quiet correction before the hook;
- ELIAN: pre-chorus lift and melodic reassembly;
- SOREN: stable central chorus statement;
- EON: upper counterline and final full-context escalation.

Why choose it:

- strongest direct fit for the concept;
- highly rap-forward without becoming one-note;
- gives the production itself a semantic role;
- closest to the momentum standard of `HUMAN ERROR` while sounding clearly different.

Risk:

Too many glitch/edit effects could make Flow sacrifice lyric intelligibility, so the final prompt would need strict limits on vocal chopping during actual lead lines.

## B — CROSSTALK

**Neo-boom-bap + jazz-rap color + modern K-pop vocal architecture**

Working tempo: approximately **98–104 BPM**, with double-time rap pockets.

Core sound:

- heavy live-feeling kick/snare pocket;
- warm bass line;
- sparse electric-piano or muted-key chords;
- dry horn-like punctuation or original brass-synth hits used sparingly;
- brief overlapping spoken textures between sections;
- deliberately roomy verses that expose cadence and internal rhyme.

Dramatic behavior:

Instead of digital corruption, distortion becomes conversational: people talking over each other, interrupting, finishing the wrong sentence, and forcing meanings onto one another. Rap sections feel like arguments in different pockets. The vocal line supplies the only moments where one complete thought is allowed to breathe.

Seven-member function:

- VALE: deliberate low-pocket opening;
- ROOK: double-time technical rebuttal;
- MIRO: syncopated speech-to-song passage;
- NOEN: intimate uninterrupted statement;
- ELIAN: harmonic lift into chorus;
- SOREN: strong melodic refrain;
- EON: upper harmony and final-response layer.

Why choose it:

- lyric and rap performance become the center of gravity;
- very different from the existing catalogue;
- leaves substantial room for personality and diction.

Risk:

Less instantly explosive than `HUMAN ERROR`; if the groove generation is weak, it could sound too laid-back for the user's requested rap-forward energy.

## C — BROKEN FEED

**Minimal trap + glitch-hop + bass-heavy contemporary K-pop**

Working tempo: approximately **136–142 BPM**, felt in halftime.

Core sound:

- deep sub-bass and sparse 808 movement;
- sharp hats with controlled burst patterns rather than constant rolls;
- hard negative space around rap lines;
- clipped digital percussion and abrupt silence as punctuation;
- short synthetic fragments that resemble feed refreshes or corrupted playback without literal notification sounds;
- chorus adds wider chords and group vocal weight while keeping the low-end dominant.

Dramatic behavior:

The first half feels unstable and constantly interrupted, as though every line is being cut before its meaning resolves. The arrangement becomes progressively less fragmented. The final section removes the interruptions almost entirely and lets the whole group land the central statement over the same beat, now fully continuous.

Seven-member function:

- VALE: sparse low-register threat / anchor;
- ROOK: high-precision rhythmic centerpiece;
- MIRO: elastic halftime melodic rap;
- NOEN: near-empty-space contrast block;
- ELIAN: melodic lift after heavy rap density;
- SOREN: broad but compact hook lead;
- EON: late upper-register tension and final release.

Why choose it:

- most aggressively rap-centered option;
- strongest rhythmic contrast between the three rap identities;
- very performance-forward.

Risk:

Could drift into generic trap unless the arrangement and lyric mechanics remain unusually specific to the quotation/context concept.

## D — REDLINE TRANSCRIPT

**Alternative hip-hop + distorted bass/guitar + live-drum K-pop**

Working tempo: approximately **106–112 BPM**.

Core sound:

- aggressive acoustic/electronic hybrid drums;
- distorted bass with short guitar stabs rather than full rock-wall chords;
- dry verses with almost no sustained harmony;
- rap phrases answered by instrumental hits;
- pre-chorus progressively opens the harmonic field;
- chorus uses full vocal power without abandoning hip-hop weight.

Dramatic behavior:

The song feels like a transcript being argued over until the paper itself catches fire metaphorically: controlled opening, escalating rebuttals, a stripped NOEN interruption, then a forceful full-context final statement. Rap remains dominant, but the last third gets the largest live-performance payoff of the four options.

Seven-member function:

- VALE: low declarative opening and punch-in rap;
- ROOK: technical centerpiece over the driest beat;
- MIRO: rhythmic transition into the melodic lift;
- NOEN: stripped interruption / truth line;
- ELIAN: pre-chorus ignition;
- SOREN: forceful chorus lead;
- EON: final upper harmony / ad-lib escalation.

Why choose it:

- strongest concert/stage energy;
- balances heavy rap with a large seven-member payoff;
- gives UNFRAME a sonic territory not yet used.

Risk:

If the generator overemphasizes guitars, the song could become rap-rock instead of rap-first K-pop. The final prompt would need to keep guitar as punctuation, not genre dominance.

## Selection lens

- **A — RECEIPT TAPE**: best concept-production integration and strongest overall fit;
- **B — CROSSTALK**: most lyric/pocket-focused and organic;
- **C — BROKEN FEED**: hardest and most rap-dominant;
- **D — REDLINE TRANSCRIPT**: biggest live-performance energy.
